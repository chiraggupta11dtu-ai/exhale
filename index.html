    Breath-hold test

    This measures your comfortable breath hold, also called the control pause. As your breathing gets calmer, the number tends to rise. Test once a week, at the same time of day.

    1. Sit still and breathe normally through your nose for a minute.
    2. After a normal, relaxed exhale, pinch your nose and tap Start hold.
    3. Tap Stop at the first clear urge to breathe. This isn't a max-effort hold.

        Techniques

        The rule behind all of them: a long, slow exhale tells your nervous system to calm down. Make the exhale longer than the inhale.

        Physiological sigh

        2 · 1 · 6

        Fastest reset, for stress right now

        Take a deep inhale through the nose, then a short second sip to fill up completely. Let it all out in a long, slow exhale through the mouth. One to three rounds bring calm in under a minute. In a 2023 Stanford study, 5 minutes a day improved mood more than mindfulness meditation did.

        Resonance breathing

        5.5 · 5.5

        Daily training

        About six breaths a minute, smooth and silent, through the nose, with the belly rising. This pace raises heart rate variability, a marker of how well your body recovers from stress. It's the core of the program.

        Box breathing

        4 · 4 · 4 · 4

        Focus under pressure

        Inhale, hold, exhale, and hold for four seconds each. Use it for two minutes before a presentation, an exam, or a hard conversation. The even count steadies attention as well as the body.

        4-7-8

        4 · 7 · 8

        Falling asleep

        Inhale quietly for four, hold for seven, and exhale through the mouth for eight. The long hold and longer exhale slow you down for bed. Start with four rounds and build up.

        Custom

        Yours

        Your own rhythm

        Set your own inhale, holds, and exhale. For calm, keep the exhale longer than the inhale. A ratio of 4 in to 6 or 8 out is a good place to start.

        If you feel dizzy or tingly, stop and breathe normally. If you have a heart or lung condition or are pregnant, keep holds short and check with your doctor first.
